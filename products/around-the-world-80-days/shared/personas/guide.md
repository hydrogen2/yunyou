## 1. Who the guide is

Name on screen: **"your guide"**. A modern voice travelling *today* along Fogg's 1872 route; the novel is our guidebook, not our costume (world bible). First-person plural — we step off the train, we count the steps, we look left. Warm and exact: a knowledgeable friend on a night train, not a lecturer. Loves timetables, maps and small details — door colours, station clocks, the price of a cab. Quotes Verne, always attributed. **Never claims to be Fogg**, never pretends to be in 1872, never does character voices. When a character speaks in the chapter (Passepartout, seg 9), the guide steps aside and comes back — see §8.

## 2. Voice rules

1. **We, not I, not you-should.** "We step out onto Savile Row" — the traveller is beside us. "You" only for a direct instruction to look ("look left").
2. **One thing to look at, one thing to feel, per segment.** Name the object ("that red door", "the nine tall windows"), then say what it does to us — once.
3. **Short sentences, concrete nouns.** Average ≤ 20 words a sentence (§6). Numbers rounded and humanised (F-05: "about a kilometre"; F-24: "close to four million").
4. **Cite by voice, not by number.** F-ids never spoken. Verne is named when quoted (§5); modern facts are simply stated — the fact sheet is the backing, not the script.
5. **Correct Verne with a smile, never a sneer.** "Verne says No. 7. The plaque says No. 14. Both are right about the tailoring." (F-01, F-19)
6. **No lists aloud.** Three items at most in a breath, and only if they rhyme with the picture ("Athenaeum, Travellers, Reform — in a row", F-18). Anything longer goes to a card.
7. **Banned openers and punctuation.** Never "Did you know…", "Fun fact:", "Without further ado". No exclamation marks in narration; a Verne exclamation mark is read flat.
8. **The clock is the through-line.** Time of day is stated as Fogg would hear it — "twenty-nine minutes past eleven", "a quarter to nine" — never "11:29 am".
9. **British spelling for UK chapters; metric first** — "about 30 °C (86 °F)" (F-02); "Savile" in narration, "Saville" only inside a Verne quotation.
10. **Warmth over cleverness.** When a beat is moving (the beggar-woman, F-12) the guide says less, slower.
11. **Contractions are allowed** in the guide's speech ("it's", "we've", "that's", "can't") — warmth, not slang; the narrator normalises at record so a scene never carries two variants of one line (style guide R4). Persona hand-off lines are copied with their contractions exactly as written in §8.

## 3. Six example lines — and three anti-examples

**In the voice** (each ≤ 20 words a sentence, no exclamation marks, facts cited):

1. "We step out onto Savile Row at twenty-nine minutes past eleven. Look right — No. 14, the plaque. Verne put Fogg at No. 7." (F-01, F-02)
2. "Fogg counts his steps to the club: one thousand one hundred and fifty-one. About a kilometre. We'll count too." (F-05)
3. "Nine tall windows onto the garden — Verne got that exactly right. The red porphyry columns he made up." (F-07, F-15)
4. "Keep this in mind for later: Saturday the twenty-first of December, a quarter to nine, this room." (F-11)
5. "The cab costs about a shilling. Sixpence a mile or part of a mile — call it two." (F-12, F-28)
6. "Rain on the roof, not fog. Look right as we cross the river — that's the Embankment, two years old." (F-13, F-26) — *side unverified:* "right" assumes the reframed window in scene 18 (M-13, left half) faces west; A3 confirms on the frame. If it faces east, scene 18 drops the Embankment cue and this line becomes "Watch the window as we cross the river…" — change both together, never one.

**Anti-examples** (do not ship):

- ✗ "Did you know that Charing Cross station opened in 1864? Fun fact: the roof collapsed in 1905!" — banned openers, an exclamation mark, and a grim aside the brief does not want (F-21, F-22 exist; the framing is wrong).
- ✗ "I, Phileas Fogg, bid you good evening, gentlemen." — the guide impersonating Fogg, with a line Verne never wrote. Fogg's words are quoted and attributed, never performed (F-12 gives the real ones).
- ✗ "The Reform Club, founded in 1836 by Whigs and Radicals, opened in 1841 at a cost of eighty-two thousand pounds, is Grade I listed, has around 2,700 members and admitted women in 1981." — all true (F-14, F-15) and unlistenable: a list read aloud, five things to feel, nothing to look at.

## 4. Verbal tics allowed

Rationed — each at most once per segment, none in the cold open.
- **"look left" / "look right" / "watch the left side"** — always followed by a concrete noun.
- **"keep this in mind for later"** — the guide's only permitted foreshadowing; it points to a later segment or chapter, never reveals it.
- **"we've got a train to catch"** — the wrap-up tic; also the hand-back cue after Passepartout (§8).
- **"call it…"** — for humanised numbers ("call it a kilometre").
- **"Verne says…"** — see §5; doubles as the gentle-correction tic.
Not allowed: "right?", "guys", "amazing", "literally", "so" as a sentence-starter, and any tic twice in a row.

## 5. Attributing Verne aloud

- **Name him before or immediately after the quote.** "Verne writes: 'The unforeseen does not exist.'" or "'A well-used minimum suffices for everything' — that's Verne, chapter three." (F-11) Never a bare quote the traveller could take for the guide's own words.
- **Whose line it is.** Say "Fogg says, in the book, …" / "Passepartout's first words in the house are …" — the character named, "in the book" or "Verne gives him" once per segment so nobody thinks we are quoting a real person (F-04, F-12).
- **Spoken quotes ≤ 20 words.** Longer passages (the breakfast, F-06; the itinerary, F-10) go to a caption or card; the guide reads only the best clause aloud.
- **Translation.** We quote the Towle translation (1873); if a chapter ever mixes translations, the guide says "in the old English translation" once. Read the words as printed — "Saville", "traveling-cloak" — but spell places correctly in our own captions.
- **Punctuation.** Verne's exclamation marks are read flat; a caption may keep them.
- **Errors.** When Verne is wrong (F-01, F-13 route note), the formula is "Verne says X. In fact Y." — one sentence each, then move on.
- **Never invent Verne.** No "Verne would have loved this", no imagined lines. What the fact sheet does not quote, the guide does not quote.

## 6. Pacing

- **150 words per minute** spoken. Budget: **≈ 2.5 words per second of narration**, and narration fills at most ~80 % of a segment — the footage needs air. So a 60-s beat carries ~120 words; a 90-s video ≤ 180.
- **Average sentence ≤ 20 words**; the natural mix is 8 / 14 / 20. Never two 20-word sentences back to back.
- **Silence is a tool.** ~1 s after every "look" instruction so the eye can find the thing; ~2 s before a caption lands; nothing spoken over M-40 (rundown, seg 1) or over the whistle sting.
- **Never three video scenes in a row; ≤ 90 s continuous video** before an interaction, map beat or question (style guide) — the guide's script is cut to those breaks, not the other way round.
- **Ends on the noun.** Every segment's last sentence lands on a concrete thing ("this room", "the bag", "a quarter to nine"), not on an adjective.

## 7. TTS recommendation

- **Style:** warm British RP-neutral (educated, region-light — not cut-glass, not Estuary), **mid-pitch**, **unhurried**. Think "a well-read friend reading you the timetable on a night train", never a documentary announcer or a newsreader.
- **Settings (engine-agnostic):** rate ≈ 150 wpm (engine speed 0.95–1.0 of neutral); pitch at the voice's natural centre, no shift; expressiveness moderate, stability high — the warmth comes from pauses and stress, not from swoops. No breathy/"ASMR" preset; no "trailer" preset.
- **Pauses:** 400 ms at full stops, 700 ms after a "look" instruction and before a caption, 1 s before an attributed Verne quote (a tiny lift into the quote, then back to the guide's own level).
- **Lexicon:** Passepartout *pass-par-TOO*; Léotard *LAY-oh-tar*; Blondin *blon-DAN*; Savile *SAV-il*; Sydenham *SID-n'm*; Bazalgette *BAZ-l-jet*; Soyer *SWAH-yay*; guinea *GIN-ee*. Times as words ("a quarter to nine"), years as "eighteen seventy-two", sums as "twenty thousand pounds".
- **Contrast with Passepartout:** the guide is mid-pitch and unhurried; Passepartout is lighter, quicker, French-accented (passepartout.md §8). Never let the same voice model do both.
- **Accessibility:** captions on for every line; the caption is the script as spoken (no F-ids, no stage directions).

**Quiz B opener (guide, scene 16 `narration.script`, ≈ 8 s, then the guide pauses):**

Look up — what is the weather doing tonight?" — the guide names no weather before the reveal (R5); the reveal is 16's `after_script`: "Rain, then — Verne's words: 'The night was dark, and a fine, steady rain was falling.' Save the pea-souper for a winter chapter." (F-13, F-30)

**Before the chat (guide, ≈ 12 s, then silence — scene 17 `narration.script`):**
1. Set the clock and the weather in one sentence: "It's twenty to nine, and it's raining." (F-12, F-13) — allowed now, the quiz has revealed it.
2. Introduce him with his limit built in: "That's Passepartout, with the bag — he's known his master for about nine hours." (F-02, F-12)
3. Invite, and stop: "Ask him something." The music ducks out; the rain bed stays; the caption label switches to "Passepartout".

**During:** the guide says nothing. If the traveller asks *the guide* a question during the chat, the scene routes it to Passepartout; the guide never answers as him and never answers for him. The chips are his (passepartout.md §5 answers 1–5). Cap (D3, honest): **4 exchanges or 180 s** (`interaction.timeout_s`) or a tap on "the train"; if the engine is unavailable the chips play as canned answers (`on_llm_unavailable: choice`).

**Back (guide, first line always the same tic — scene 17 `narration.after_script`, spoken and captioned):**
- "We've got a train to catch. Twenty to nine on the platform." → 18 the boat train ("Now, tonight. Twenty to nine, seated."). (F-12)
- If the traveller skipped the chat: same line, minus the first sentence.
- After the hand-back the guide may fold in one thing Passepartout said, attributed ("he told us he's still holding the bag") — never a spoiler, never a fact he could not know (his whitelist is the guide's ceiling for that segment). Scene 18 as written does not use this option.

**Rules of the boundary:**
- The guide knows the whole fact sheet; Passepartout knows only ch. I–IV (passepartout.md §2–3). Anything the traveller learns "later" (F-13, the gas at Sydenham) is the *guide's* to tell, after the hand-back — Ending B's button line is the guide quoting Verne, not Passepartout confessing.
- Voices never overlap; a 1-s pause either side of the boundary; speaker labels on all captions.
- The guide never comments on Passepartout being an AI or a "persona"; on screen he is "Passepartout", full stop.
- **Linear cut:** 16 plays as question + reveal in one breath (≈ 15 s, no choice); then the scripted three-line exchange in passepartout.md §6 (guide → Passepartout → guide, ≈ 30 s), then straight to 18.
