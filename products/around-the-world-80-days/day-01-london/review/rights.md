## Platform terms that shape the build (verified 2026-08-18)

**YouTube — API Services Developer Policies & Required Minimum Functionality (RMF).** The embedded player counts as an API Service, so these apply to any product that embeds:
- III.F.3.a: "API Clients must not charge users to watch content in an embedded YouTube player"; III.F.3.b: must not "gate access to a video by requiring a user to take an action other than clicking the play button". → **A paywalled Yunyou chapter whose scenes are YouTube embeds is on the wrong side of this.** Options: (i) chapters with embeds are free, paid tier sells the interactive extras/souvenirs/linear cut made from *licensed* footage; (ii) license footage directly from creators (outreach below) and self-host — then no YouTube policy applies; (iii) keep embeds but do not sell access to the chapter. Decision 1 below.
- RMF "Overlays and frames": "You must not display overlays, frames, or other visual elements in front of any part of a YouTube embedded player, including player controls." → Scenes 02, 05, 06, 13, 18 put pins/captions at `bl`/`bottom`. They must render in the app's own chrome (letterbox band, side panel, or below the player on fold-open), **not** on top of the iframe. Same for the "crop/zoom to the left half" idea for M-13 (III.I.6 "modify, build upon, or block any portion or functionality of a YouTube player").
- RMF: player ≥ 200×200 px; only one autoplaying player per screen; autoplay only when >50 % visible; III.F.1.3 do not obscure YouTube attribution; III.E.1.a no download/cache/store of audiovisual content. Playing our own narration/ambience *alongside* the embed is fine (III.I forbids separating YouTube's audio from its video, not adding ours).
- Standard YouTube Terms: uploaders who enable embedding grant YouTube/its users the right to display through the embeddable player; a creator's "I do not license my content" statement does not override that, but they can switch embedding off or delete at any time — hence one backup per primary (already in the manifest).

**Google Maps Platform Terms.** §3.2.2(b): "Customer will display all attribution that Google provides… Customer will not modify, obscure, or delete such attribution." §3.2.3(a) No scraping: no "pre-fetch, index, store, reshare, or rehost"; (b) No caching except as the Service Specific Terms allow; (c) No creating content from Google Maps Content; (d) product must have "substantial, independent value" beyond Google's (ours does). Maps Embed API: "available at no charge… unlimited requests", "you must include an API key with all API requests". EEA-billed customers fall under the separate EEA ToS. → Amber for M-37/38/39: create the project + key, keep attribution visible, no recording panos into the linear cut, no offline bundles.

**Wikimedia Commons licences in a commercial product.** PD and CC0: no obligation (we credit anyway). CC BY 2.0/4.0: credit creator, title, licence (link), and note changes — commercial use fine. CC BY-SA 2.0/3.0: same, plus any *adaptation* must be released under the same licence — an unmodified still inside a larger work is a collection, not an adaptation, so the chapter itself is not infected; a crop/tint/composite is.
**[A6 — rule retired 2026-08-19.]** The old rule ("BY-SA images ship only unmodified and stay out of generated composites") existed because the studio's own output licence was still an open question. **D4 answered it: our outputs are CC BY-SA 4.0.** So adapting BY-SA material now costs us nothing. New rule: **BY-SA material may be cropped, tinted, Ken-Burnsed and composited, provided the output ships under CC BY-SA 4.0 with attribution and the ShareAlike notice** (CC BY-SA 4.0 §3(b) requires the Adapter's Licence to be stated and linked). CC BY-SA **2.0** §4(b) permits releasing a derivative under "a later version of this License with the same License Elements", so the 2.0 stills (M-20, M-21, M-33, M-69, M-77a/b) may be adapted and released under BY-SA **4.0** — one licence for the whole episode. Every BY-SA-derived asset carries `sa: true` on its media record, so a future change to D4 yields a strip-list. **Hard stop unchanged: no NC and no ND** — an NC component is incompatible with our own BY-SA promise of commercial reuse downstream, *even while D1 keeps us free*. Full reasoning: `rights-a6.md` §2.

## Rights policy suggestions for the studio
- **Prefer CC0/PD, then CC BY, then CC BY-SA (unmodified only), never NC/ND for a commercial product**; record the licence *as verified* (Commons API/freesound page), not as remembered.
- **Always credit, even when not required** (CC0, PD, Rijksmuseum, pdsounds): a per-chapter credits screen plus in-scene source lines; it is cheap and it is what creators remember.
- **Embed-only for platform video** (YouTube standard player, Google Maps Embed API): no downloads, no re-hosting, no frame grabs, no screen recordings, no overlays on the player, one backup per primary because embeds are revocable.
- **Anything we sell must be built on assets we may sell**: licensed or owned footage for paid tiers/linear cuts; embeds and Street View stay in the free, interactive layer until licences exist.
- **Keep an attribution manifest per chapter** (machine-readable: id, title, creator, licence, URL, changes, scenes) generated from the scene JSON `attribution` fields; the credits screen and this review are rendered from it, so nothing ships uncredited.
