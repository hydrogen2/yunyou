## 0 · What changed, in one paragraph

The old chapter was a walk through a novel: places appeared because Verne put something there, and the guide
talked almost continuously over stills. This film is a walk through **three places in half a mile of London**, each
of which is worth twenty minutes on its own — a 280-metre street that owns the word "suit", an institution that let
a man with a small house live in a palace, and the paving stone that Britain measures its road distances from — set
in the one year, **1872**, when the planet finished wiring itself shut. Phileas Fogg is the thread that runs through
all three: he is teased at 0:27 and the story proper starts at **4:47**, after the street has earned him.

**The single strongest fact we own is F-141** (the Australian Overland Telegraph closed the last gap on 22 August
1872, six weeks before Fogg leaves). It reframes the whole wager: he races the ships, but the news about him travels
instantly. It is teased in the cold open and paid off in full as segment S8, the hinge of the film.

## 1 · The laws this film is cut to

Give these to the Scene Developer and the Engine as hard constraints; every beat below already obeys them.

**L1 · Shots, not sentences.** A picture changes when the eye tires, not when a sentence ends.
- **No still image stays on screen longer than 12 s** without a cut or a slow move (push / pan across a detail).
- **Motion shots run 8–20 s**, archive film up to 20 s, and nothing at all runs past 30 s.
- Beats below that hold three plates say so ("3 cuts") — the *beat* is 18 s, the *shots* are 6 s each.
- Average time between visual changes across the film: **≈ 8 s** (many beats cut internally). 88 beats, ~110 individual shots, in 1,184 s.

**L2 · Narration budget — the fix for "heavily narrated".**
- **≤ 1,700 words for the whole film**, giving **≈ 60 % fill** (the guide speaks about 11½ minutes of 19¾).
  Per-segment word caps are in each header. The Narrator writes to the cap; if a beat cannot be said inside it, the
  beat loses a fact, not a second of picture.
- **18 marked silences** (listed per beat as **HOLD**). They are content, not gaps (`positioning.md`, "the chill").
- Nothing spoken over the telegraph reveal's last 8 s, the whistle, or the beggar-woman.

**L3 · Text on screen — no floating anything (D9).** Only two legal forms:
1. **Burned captions** (the subtitle track) — they already do the job of a lower-third.
2. **Designed type**: a **full-frame card** (cut to it, like a title page in a book) or type that is *drawn into* a
   map. One exception is requested below and flagged for approval: an **opaque full-width band** locked to the
   bottom edge, used only to date archive footage honestly ("London, 1903") and for the three cold-open place names.
   It is opaque, static and lasts the whole shot — it does not hover, animate or point. **If the founder vetoes the
   band, those seven uses become full-frame cards** and the film loses 6 s. No pins. No lower-thirds. Ever.

**L4 · Map spec (D9).** Big type, **four labels maximum on screen at once**, high contrast, revealed over time,
legible on a 6-inch phone at arm's length. Every map in this film draws itself; none is presented complete.

**L5 · Quiz spec (D9).** **Pose → hold → reveal.** The options never render with the answer marked. There is
**one quiz in the film** (S2), it is about a word rather than the novel, and the guide states the answer only in the
reveal (style guide R5). Recommendation and the case for zero quizzes are in Decisions.

**L6 · House look, protected (D9).** Period sound design and historic prints/engravings are the spine, not the
fallback: 26 of the 86 shots are engravings, plates or period photographs, and the sound bed never drops out.

**L7 · Folklore.** `places.md` lists seven stories that must never be narrated as true. Two of them appear in this
film **and both are named as folklore and then refuted on screen** (bespoke, S2; *chère reine*, S9). The other five
do not appear at all. See §7, Folklore compliance.

**L8 · Voice.** One guide, `shared/personas/guide.md`, clear English only (D8). One optional second voice
(Passepartout, 12 s, S10) — see Decisions.

## 2 · Shape

**Hook (first 30 seconds):** three real places in half a mile, each stated as a fact a stranger would want, then a
world map wiring itself shut in August 1872 — *then* the man who bet half his fortune on that year. The title card
lands at **0:36**, after the reason to stay.

**Three acts.**
- **Act I — The street (S1–S3, 5:14).** One 280-metre street owns a word, lost that word in a tribunal in 2008, and
  answered by defining the word with a tape measure. At the bottom of it, in 1872, sat the headquarters of British
  exploration — and six doors up, a novelist put a man who was about to go round the world.
- **Act II — The institution (S4–S7, 6:55).** Why the quarter exists (a palace), what a club actually *was* (a
  second home you were elected into), why *this* club exists (a political weapon built as a palazzo), and the wager.
  **The turn:** "The train leaves for Dover at a quarter before nine."
- **Act III — The circuit (S8–S11, 7:35).** Why 1872 and not 1852: a port, twelve termini, an underground railway,
  and a wire that closed around the planet six weeks before he left. Then the place he leaves from — which is not
  a station, it is the point London is measured from — and a quarter to nine.

**Balance:** roughly **68 % place, 32 % novel** by seconds. Fogg is teased at 0:27, and the novel-thread proper starts at 4:47 (S3.5). He is never on screen for
longer than 20 s without a real place under him.
