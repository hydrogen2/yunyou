# Positioning — 读万卷书，行万里路 (founder, 2026-08-20)

**What Yunyou is:** guided journeys through the places that stories made famous — where the work is the reason to
care about the place, and the place is the excuse to finally meet the work.

**Motto / internal name:** 读万卷书，行万里路 — *read ten thousand books, travel ten thousand miles.* Both halves are
unhurried by nature; that is not decoration, it is the tone spec (see "The chill" below).

## The insight this rests on
The founder's own reaction to Day 1: *"more attractive than I'd thought — maybe because I'm interested in the novel
and always wanted to know what it's about."* Almost everyone carries a list of famous works they feel they should
know, have never read, and quietly intend to. That is the hook — **finally finding out what the famous thing is
about, by going to where it happened** — and it is far more specific than "travel + education".

It also dissolves the tension between the 文艺 half and the informational half: the art supplies the *emotional*
reason to care, the facts supply the *substance*. Rick Steves has facts without a story; literary video has story
without a place. The gap between them is the product — Day 1's best beat is exactly that gap ("Verne got the address
wrong, and it doesn't matter").

## The grid: works × places
The unit of production is **a work × a place**, not an episode in a series. That makes a grid, not a list:
- collect a **place** across works (Paris through several authors), or
- collect a **work** across places (Verne around the globe).

Consequences:
- **"What's next?" is generated, not chosen.** The theme produces candidates forever; taste only ranks them.
- **The collector instinct is served structurally** — a grid invites completion in a way a feed never does. The
  interface should eventually show the shelf/map of what you have travelled. (Product backlog, not a chapter.)
- *Around the World in 80 Days* is therefore **the first work in a collection**, not "season one of a travel show".

## Scope rule — an identity must exclude things
In: **works that made a real place famous.** Sherlock's London · Dracula's Whitby–Transylvania · Journey to the West
along the real Silk Road · the Odyssey's Mediterranean · Les Misérables' Paris.
Out: abstract art, pure invention with no real anchor, and "culture" in general. Wholly invented worlds enter only
through their real analogues — which is also the legally safe door (see `worlds-ladder.md` rung 3).

## Rights ladder — public domain first, protected works slowly and carefully
1. **Public domain now** (Verne, Homer, Dante, Journey to the West, Sherlock in most jurisdictions — verify per work
   and per territory). We may quote freely and use period illustration as *material*.
2. **CC / permissioned** next.
3. **Protected works later, carefully**, and only under the documentary posture below. Never as a first move, never
   as material. Rights reviews per title before a chapter is briefed — not after it is written.

## The documentary posture (how protected works are handled)
For a work still in copyright the work becomes our **subject**, never our **media**: we visit the real places,
discuss the work, quote briefly and with attribution. No film clips, no stills lifted from the work, no reproduction
of substantial text. Practically this means film-based chapters look different from Verne's — more present-day
footage and archive, less imagery *from* the work — and the craft must carry that rather than fight it.

## The chill — tone is a hard constraint, not a mood
The founder: *"the chill of reading and travelling, the two most enjoyable activities."* This is a spec, and it
outranks engagement tactics:
- **No urgency.** No cliffhangers, no "stay tuned", no manufactured stakes. The traveller may stop at any time.
- **Silence is content.** Ambience and held shots are allowed to breathe; the guide need not fill every second.
  (Day 1 evidence: the scenes that failed the density check were the ones talking non-stop.)
- **Never school.** Quizzes must feel like curiosity, not assessment — soft, skippable, teaching in the feedback
  rather than scoring. Anything that feels like a test breaks the posture.
- **Slower than platform norms.** Pacing follows the walk and the page, not the algorithm.

## What this changes in the studio
- **Rundown Writer:** briefs are `work × place`; the spine is the gap between the imagined and the real.
- **Researcher:** reads the work *and* the place; the fact sheet must carry both.
- **Rights:** clears the *work* (PD status per territory) before a chapter is briefed, not at review time.
- **Continuity:** owns the house voice — unhurried, warm, exact — across works, so a Dante chapter and a Verne
  chapter feel like the same guide.
- **Scene types:** `timeline` (then ↔ now) is the single most on-theme type we have — imagined versus real is
  literally the product. `window` gains meaning as stepping into a room that only ever existed on a page.

Supersedes nothing in `worlds-ladder.md`; it fixes rung 2 as the permanent identity rather than a stage, and makes
rung 3 an occasional guest reached through real anchors.
