# VIDEO FIRST — the founder's reorientation, 2026-09-08

**The video is the product.** There may never be a player. A chapter is a **film**, and a "scene" is a **shot list**,
not an interactive unit that happens to be rendered. Nothing is designed for interaction and then flattened.
This supersedes every player-shaped assumption in the schema, the roles and the templates.

> *"you need to forget the /player version now and get fully oriented to the video version… video is never a
> rendering of the /player because there may never be a /player."*

## Protect these — they are working (founder, after watching Day 1)
1. **Period sound design.** "Gives an old school movie feel." Keep and extend it.
2. **Historic printmaking, engravings and illustrations.** "A key differentiator from a pure video tour — gives it
   more of a book-reading feel." This is the house look. Lean into it rather than treating prints as a fallback
   for missing footage.

## Fix these
3. **Not enough visuals; it plays as heavily narrated, which gets tiring.** The picture must carry its share.
   Every claim wants something to look at, and a shot should change before the eye tires of it — not when the
   sentence happens to end.
4. **Source real video for places.** Stills alone cannot carry a place. Where a place is discussed, find motion.
5. **NO floating text overlays.** No lower-thirds, no pins, no floating captions. They are distracting, hard to
   read, and **captions already do that job**. Text on screen = burned captions + type that is part of a designed
   card or map. Nothing hovering.
6. **Scene types must be VIDEO-friendly**, not ports of interactive ideas:
   - **Map:** type large and high-contrast, few labels, revealed over time. It must read at a glance on a phone,
     not reward squinting. Day 1's map is "very small, low contrast, hard to read, too much text" — that is the anti-pattern.
   - **Quiz:** a film cannot take an answer. Pose the question, hold on the picture, *then* reveal. Day 1 "gives away
     the answer from the start" — the options render with the correct one already marked. Never do that.
   - Anything that only works with a tap (tap-to-find, drag, chat, walk) is either redesigned as a watchable beat or cut.

## The script fix that matters most: strengthen the PLACE axis
> *"why would I care about savile row if theres nothing special there? things like dover and calais are the two
> port endpoints on the channel may account as a general knowledge to me"*

Day 1's scripts are **work-centric**: places appear because Verne put something there. That inverts the product.
`positioning.md` says a work × place grid — the work is the *thread*, the places are the *substance*.

**Rule: every place must earn its screen time independently of the novel.** Before a place appears, answer plainly:
*what is this place, and why would someone with no interest in the novel find it worth knowing?* Give the general
knowledge an educated person would be glad to have — then let the novel's thread run through it.

Worked examples from the founder:
- **Savile Row** — not "where Verne put Fogg's house", but the home of bespoke tailoring; why the world's suits are
  named after this one short street.
- **The Reform Club** — what a London gentlemen's club actually *was*, as an institution.
- **Charing Cross** — why it matters to London, not merely that Fogg left from it.
- **London itself** — what kind of city this was in 1872; scale, novelty, why it was the place a bet like this starts.
- **Dover and Calais** — the two ports facing each other across the Channel: general knowledge worth having.

## Consequences for the studio
- **Rundown Writer** plans a film: shots, durations, what is on screen every second. Place-first, work-threaded.
- **Content Preparer** hunts motion for every place beat, and keeps the print/engraving spine that is working.
- **Scene Developer** stops authoring interactions. Overlays are retired from the film entirely.
- **Engine** removes overlay rendering from the linear cut, and rebuilds the map and quiz beats as film devices.
- The player, if it ever returns, renders the film's content. It is downstream, not upstream.
