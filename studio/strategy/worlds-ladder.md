# Strategy — the Worlds Ladder (founder + Chief, 2026-08-19)

Yunyou tours are place-themed, but "place" spans three rungs. Each rung is a different production problem;
the studio climbs deliberately, proving the engine on each rung before the next.

## Rung 1 — Real places, no narrative spine
"Kyoto in 5 days." Easiest media (walking video, Street View), weakest arc. We use rung-1 *technique* everywhere
but don't lead with rung-1 products: the curation moat is thin.

## Rung 2 — Fiction anchored in real places  ← CURRENT (Around the World in 80 Days)
The narrative engine of a novel + the full real-world media supply. The editorial voice lives in the *gap* between
fiction and reality ("Verne got the address wrong — and it doesn't matter"). The world bible line that defines rung 2:
**we travel today; the novel is our guidebook, not our costume.**
Candidates after 80 Days: Sherlock's London · Dracula (Whitby–Transylvania) · Journey to the West (real Silk Road) ·
Les Misérables' Paris · the Odyssey's Mediterranean. Pick by audience: Sherlock (Western) vs Journey to the West (Chinese) first.

## Rung 3 — Fully invented worlds
Strongest narrative, no footage. **Doctrine: illustration is the correct medium here, not a fallback.**
The genre lived for a century as text + plates + maps; under-specified images recruit the viewer's imagination as the
renderer (the founding "cloud travelling" insight). Photoreal competes with the films and loses; painterly co-renders.

### Rung-3 media doctrine
1. **Public-domain illustration first.** Doré (Inferno 135 plates, Paradise Lost, Ancient Mariner), Tenniel (Alice),
   Denslow (Oz), mythology painting, Chinese woodcuts (Journey to the West). Museum open access: Rijksmuseum, Met, Getty.
   (Day 1 London already uses this technique: the Neuville/Benett plates.)
2. **AI-generated gap-fill,** under two disciplines:
   - *Style consistency:* per-world style bible + a locked generation recipe anchored to the PD tradition
     ("engraving after Doré", never "epic fantasy art"). Owner: Continuity Editor + Content Preparer.
   - *Provenance:* Rights reviews generated assets like any media — no mimicry of living artists, no franchise
     designs laundered through a model (a "Hogwarts-like castle" is still radioactive). Public-domain worlds only, at first.
3. **Scene types shift:** photo/Ken-Burns-over-plates, map, ambience and `window` become the workhorses
   (generate the five cube faces of an interior → a parallax room no camera could film). Video/streetview drop out.
4. **Roles shift:** Researcher reads the source text + scholarship (lore, not Wikipedia); Fact-Checker becomes
   lore-checker (citations = canto/chapter); Content Preparer becomes part art-director.

### Pilot sketch — Dante's Inferno (public domain, zero IP risk)
- Built-in itinerary: nine circles → nine chapters (+ prologue "the dark wood", epilogue "we emerge to see the stars").
- Guide already in the text: Virgil (dialogue persona, text-bound like Passepartout); Dante as the traveller = the viewer.
- Media: Doré plates as the visual spine; Botticelli's map of Hell; generated gap-fill in engraving style; ambience beds.
- Educative layer: medieval Florence, the real people in each circle, theology, a little Italian (terza rima, one tercet a day).
- Souvenirs: a tercet card per day; the Botticelli map assembled circle by circle as the drip progresses.
- Exercises every rung-3 problem at minimal cost; deliberately *small* (9 short chapters, ~12 min each).

## Sequence (proposal for PLAN.md when production resumes)
1. Finish 80 Days (rung 2, proves the engine end to end).
2. Second rung-2 title (repeatability; pick by target audience).
3. Inferno pilot (rung 3 pipeline: illustration-led scenes, generation recipe, lore-checking) — can start as a
   weekend/C-track experiment before 80 Days finishes.
4. Only then evaluate licensed universes (Tolkien estate, Wizarding World) as *partnerships*, never as scraping.
